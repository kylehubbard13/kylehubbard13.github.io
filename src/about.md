# About

I pride myself on being the bridge between the technically minded and the business
focused. I enjoy a heads-down build session, but I'm just as interested in the "why" behind
what we build and who it's for.

I studied Mathematics and Business Administration at Gonzaga University, which is where I
first got hooked on using software to solve business problems.

My career started at Fast Enterprises, where I spent seven years on four deployments that
replaced the Driver and Motor Vehicle systems for Washington and Maryland. I started out
building transactions for the people working the counters, and over time I moved into
running subsystems, training developers, and eventually managing a team of 14 developers and
3 subject matter experts at the Maryland MVA (while still doing implementation work myself).
Those years honed my technical skills and showed me how much a successful deployment
depends on the stakeholders around it.

In 2022 I moved to Black Duck, where I led deployments of Black Duck SCA and Polaris for 73
customers. Working inside that many development teams broadened my perspective a lot, and
it got me in the habit of bringing what I heard from customers back to our product team.

In April 2026 I joined TrendAI's Global Alliances team, where I work on the technical side
of our {% term "hyperscaler", "hyperscaler" %} partnerships, mostly AWS. Most of my time goes
to AI security, and specifically how customers keep control of AI agents once they're in
production: what an agent is allowed to touch, what it can act on, and where a person needs
to stay in the loop. I spend a lot of that time explaining what Vision One, our cloud and AI
security platform, can do in terms that make sense to both engineers and executives. I also
built TrendAI Co-Sell IQ, which AWS highlighted at the Marketplace Sellers Conference and on
AWS Partnerships re:Imagined.

I've always thought of myself as the bridge between the room that's debugging the code and
the room that's deciding whether to fund it. Alliances turned out to be a natural fit,
since it's the same job between two companies instead of two departments.

Outside of work, I joined the
[Denver Kids Young Professionals Council](https://denverkidsinc.org/our-team/young-professionals-council-ypc/)
in July 2025. Denver Kids works with Denver students facing adversity to help them finish
high school and move on to college or a career, and the council supports that through
fundraising, events, and recruiting mentors. Meeting other young professionals around Denver
is a nice bonus. It also brings me back to Gonzaga, where I mentored a local student through
Campus Kids.
