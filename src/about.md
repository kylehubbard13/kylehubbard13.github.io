# About

Right now, I'm watching agentic AI move from pilot to production on AWS, and figuring out how to secure that shift is most of my day. I'm a Solutions Architect at TrendAI, working within our Global Alliances team across our hyperscaler partnerships, with the bulk of my time focused on AWS.

Most of that work centers on AI Security: specifically, how you govern an AI agent once it's live, deciding what it's allowed to touch, what it can act on, and where a human needs to stay in the loop. As more of our AWS customers put agents into production, that governance question is becoming the center of the conversation, and I spend my time translating what Vision One can do there into terms that land with both engineers and executives.

Before TrendAI, I spent two years at Black Duck as a Customer Delivery Engineer, leading deployments for Black Duck SCA and Polaris clients. That role broadened my perspective on software development and deployment significantly, and gave me a front-row seat to secure development practices and how tools actually integrate into a client's development lifecycle. It also drove home how much client value comes from pushing product teams toward client-focused enhancements, not just implementing what already exists.

My career started at Fast Enterprises, where I spent 7 years and played a crucial role in four major deployments that revolutionized the Driver and Motor Vehicle software systems for Washington (8 million residents) and Maryland (6.2 million residents). I built programs that were intuitive for end-users, compliant with state regulations, and performant at scale. As I progressed, I managed entire subsystems, trained new developers, led a team through post-launch support, and worked closely with executive stakeholders to keep project milestones on track. Those years honed my technical skills and underscored the importance of stakeholder engagement for successful software deployment.

I studied Mathematics and Business Administration at Gonzaga University, which is where I first found the pull toward solving business problems through software.

I've always thought of myself as the bridge between the room that's debugging the code and the room that's deciding whether to fund it, translating in both directions so nothing gets lost in between.
