# About

I've spent most of my career working across an organizational boundary rather than inside
one. Different employers, different industries, same job underneath: be the person both
sides can ask a direct question and get a straight answer.

Right now that's alliances. I'm a Solutions Architect on TrendAI's Global Alliances team,
working across our hyperscaler partnerships with the bulk of my time on AWS. Most of it
centers on AI security &mdash; specifically, how you govern an agent once it's live:
deciding what it's allowed to touch, what it can act on, and where a human needs to stay in
the loop. As more of our AWS customers put agents into production, that governance question
has become the center of the conversation, and my job is translating what Vision One does
there into terms that land with engineers and executives both.

Before TrendAI I spent nearly four years at Black Duck leading deployments of Black Duck SCA
and Polaris &mdash; 73 of them. That work put me inside 73 different companies' development
lifecycles, which is a fast way to learn how much client value comes from pushing product
teams toward what customers actually need, rather than implementing what already exists.

My career started at Fast Enterprises, where I spent seven years on four major deployments
that rebuilt the Driver and Motor Vehicle systems for Washington (8 million residents) and
Maryland (6.2 million). I wrote code that had to be intuitive for counter staff, compliant
with state regulation, and fast enough for 44,000 transactions a day. As I moved up I ran
subsystems, trained developers, led a team through post-launch support, and sat in weekly
prioritization meetings with executive stakeholders. Those years taught me that the
technical work is maybe half of what makes a deployment succeed.

I studied Mathematics and Business Administration at Gonzaga, which is where I first found
the pull toward solving business problems with software &mdash; and probably where the
two-registers habit started.

I've always thought of myself as the bridge between the room that's debugging the code and
the room that's deciding whether to fund it, translating in both directions so nothing gets
lost in between. Alliances is just the version of that job with two companies instead of two
departments.
